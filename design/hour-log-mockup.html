Fixing it

Correcting a day almost never means “let me scroll a time picker.” It means the app caught you at the gas station instead of the site. So it offers the times it actually observed first, and keeps the picker as the fallback.

Why orange only appears twice

Hi-vis orange marks days past nine hours, and the one button that commits a record. Nothing else earns it. If you see orange, money was on the line — that’s the whole legend, and you never have to learn it.

Why the numbers are monospaced

Every hour figure and timestamp is set in mono with tabular figures, so a column of fourteen days lines up on the decimal. Spotting the odd day becomes a glance rather than a read. Mono also quietly says “this is a recording, not an opinion.”

Why there are no cards

A log book is ruled lines. Fourteen floating cards with shadows would break the one thing the period screen is for — running your eye down a single column of hours without interruption.

Every time says where it came from

“Scheduled,” “detected,” “on site.” A number the app guessed and a number you confirmed are not the same kind of fact, and it should never blur them — least of all the one you might have to defend.

Arriving and starting are two clocks

Your phone sees you at 6:47; you’re paid from 7:00. The hours are built from the scheduled start, never from arrival — otherwise every day inflates a little. Arrival is kept underneath as backup, and earns its keep as a tripwire: show up at 4:52 for an early call-in and the app knows this isn’t a normal day, so it asks instead of assuming.

No lunch anywhere

You work through and get paid through, so there is no break to subtract and no timer to start. One fewer thing on screen, and one fewer thing to get wrong.
